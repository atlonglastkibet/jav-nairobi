# Nairobi Constituency Poverty Rates Documentation

**Source:** Stats Kenya - Commission on Revenue Allocation (CRA)

**Year:** 2019  
**Geographic Level:** Constituency (administrative unit between county and ward)

## What is Poverty Rate?

The **poverty headcount rate** is the proportion (percentage) of the population living below the national poverty line. It measures the share of people whose income or consumption is insufficient to meet basic needs.

### National Context (2021 Kenya Household Continuous Survey):
- **Overall poverty rate:** 38.6% of Kenyans live below the poverty line
- **Food poverty rate:** 30.5% cannot afford adequate food
- **Hardcore poverty rate:** 5.8% are in extreme poverty
- **Nairobi County overall:** 16.5% (lowest poverty rate in Kenya)

## What the Numbers Mean

The poverty rate percentage indicates the **proportion of people in that constituency living in poverty**.

### Examples:
- **Kibra: 26.3%** → 26.3% of Kibra residents live below the poverty line (roughly 1 in 4 people)
- **Embakasi West: 6.4%** → Only 6.4% live in poverty (roughly 1 in 16 people)

### Interpretation:
- **Higher percentage = More poverty = Lower socioeconomic status**
- **Lower percentage = Less poverty = Higher socioeconomic status**

## Classification for Equity Analysis

Based on the poverty rates, constituencies can be classified:

| Income Class | Poverty Rate Range | Constituencies |
|--------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Low Income** | ≥18% poverty | Kibra (26.3%), Starehe (20.0%), Dagoretti South (18.5%) |
| **Middle Income** | 11-17.9% poverty | Westlands, Mathare, Embakasi South, Dagoretti North, Langata, Roysambu, Ruaraka, Kasarani |
| **High Income** | <11% poverty | Kamukunji, Embakasi North/East/Central/West, Makadara |

## Key Insights for Transit Equity Analysis

### Poverty Distribution in Nairobi:
1. **Kibra** has the highest poverty rate (26.3%) - home to Africa's largest urban informal settlement
2. **Mathare** (14.5%) - another major informal settlement
3. **Embakasi constituencies** have the lowest poverty rates (6.4-7.5%)
4. **Range:** 6.4% to 26.3% - a 4x difference between richest and poorest areas

### For Transit Equity:
- Compare transit coverage between high-poverty areas (Kibra, Mathare, Starehe) and low-poverty areas (Embakasi, Westlands)
- **Pro-poor transit system:** Higher coverage in high-poverty constituencies
- **Inequitable system:** Higher coverage in low-poverty (affluent) constituencies

### Administrative Note:
- Kenya has **290 constituencies** nationwide
- Nairobi has **17 constituencies**
- Constituencies are larger than wards but smaller than counties
- Each constituency is governed by a Member of Parliament (MP)

## Using This Data

To apply this data to your ward-level analysis:

1. **Map wards to constituencies** (wards are subdivisions of constituencies)
2. **Assign poverty rate** from constituency to its component wards
3. **Classify income level** based on poverty thresholds
4. **Compare transit coverage** across income classes

### Example Constituency-Ward Mapping:
- **Kibra Constituency** includes wards: Makina, Lindi, Laini Saba, Soweto East, Silanga
- **Mathare Constituency** includes wards: Mathare North, Hospital, Kiamaiko, Huruma
- **Westlands Constituency** includes wards: Karura, Parklands/Highridge, Kitisuru, Mountain View

## Data Limitations

1. **Constituency-level data:** More aggregated than ward-level; assumes uniform poverty within constituencies
2. **2019 data:** May not reflect recent changes (though poverty typically changes slowly)
3. **Income proxy:** Poverty rate is one dimension of socioeconomic status; doesn't capture wealth inequality within areas
4. **Urban complexity:** Nairobi has mixed-income neighborhoods; some constituencies contain both informal settlements and middle-class areas
